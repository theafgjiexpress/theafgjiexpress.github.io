Top story

AFGJI Students wreak havoc at Jodhpur, Jim Corbett

Photo placeholder

No image attached

The Class XII batch spent four days in Jodhpur, returning with reports of two broken beds, a damaged lift and broken lights at the accommodation. The circumstances surrounding the reported damage remain unclear, and it has not been established whether all the incidents were deliberate.

Meanwhile, the Class X trip to Jim Corbett has drawn attention following claims that seven lamps were broken during the excursion. The allegation is currently being verified, and the exact extent of the damage remains unconfirmed.

“students encountered accompanying teachers allegedly under the influence of intoxicating substances”

The incidents have also raised concerns about teacher conduct during the trips. Reports from the excursions suggest that students encountered accompanying teachers allegedly under the influence of intoxicating substances. The circumstances and details of these reports have yet to be independently established.

News

Disciplinary Action at School

The matter was addressed on Thursday, when the Class XII students were assembled in the dance room. Mohini Ma'am reprimanded the batch over its conduct during the Jodhpur trip, addressing concerns about misbehaviour and the reported damage to hotel property. She, along with Alka Ma'am, went on to make derogatory comments about the way the girls who attended the Jodhpur trip were dressed.

A separate incident involving Shilpa Ma'am also attracted attention after a student, whose identity is being withheld, uploaded a video showing an argument between a student and the teacher. Following the confrontation, Mohini Ma'am reportedly reprimanded the student involved in the argument.

News

Students at AFGJI Form Underground Journalism Page

In an unexpected development, students at AFGJI have launched an independent journalism page aimed at reporting school happenings, student opinions and stories that might otherwise go unnoticed. Operating outside the school's official communication channels, the initiative seeks to offer a fresh perspective on campus life.

With its first issue, the publication marks the beginning of an attempt to give students a platform to observe, question and document their surroundings. Although whether it becomes a lasting voice for the student community remains to be seen.
